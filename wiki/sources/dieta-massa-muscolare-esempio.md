# Esempio dieta per aumentare la massa muscolare

**Source**: [raw/Esempio dieta per aumentare la massa muscolare.md](../raw/Esempio%20dieta%20per%20aumentare%20la%20massa%20muscolare.md)
**Date**: 2025-10-14
**Type**: Article
**Author**: Dott. Riccardo Borgacci

## Key Takeaways
- Muscle mass diet should only be done if body fat ≤12-14% (men)
- Hypercaloric diet: +10% calories or +100-500kcal surplus
- Surplus mainly from carbohydrates, lower fat intake
- Protein: 1.6-1.8 g/kg, not exceeding 2.2 g/kg
- Duration varies based on fat gain — stop when fat increases excessively
- Meal timing important, especially post-workout
- Example: 23-year-old male, 2350 kcal with specific meal plan provided
- Supplements: creatine, beta-alanine, protein powder, dextrose

## Summary

This article provides a comprehensive guide to muscle mass diets, which are hypercaloric regimens designed to facilitate muscle hypertrophy when combined with resistance training. The author emphasizes that such diets should only be undertaken if body fat does not exceed 12-14% (for men), as excessive body fat reduces insulin sensitivity and increases the risk of gaining too much fat mass.

The diet must meet several essential requirements: nutritional balance with adequate vitamins, minerals, water, and fiber; caloric intake 10% above normal needs (or +100-500kcal); duration that is relative rather than standardized — the diet should be stopped when fat gain becomes excessive or when the surplus reduces insulin sensitivity. The caloric surplus should come primarily from carbohydrates, with lower fat intake (both percentage and absolute terms). Protein intake should be "adequate," typically 1.6-1.8 g/kg without exceeding 2.2 g/kg. Intelligent meal fractionation is important, with emphasis on post-workout nutrition.

The article provides a detailed example for a 23-year-old male student (172cm, 65.8kg, BMI 22.2) who trains 3 times weekly. The strategy is hypercaloric +10% (2350 kcal), with fats at 15% of total calories, protein at 2.0 g/kg, and the remainder as carbohydrates. If insufficient, increase by 25g of carbohydrates every 7-10 days. A complete daily meal plan is provided with specific quantities and food substitutions.

For very high-calorie diets, the article recommends estimating fat intake at 0.5 g/kg for men and 0.8 g/kg for women, with minimums of 30-35 g/day for men and 40-60 g/day for women to avoid deficiencies in fat-soluble vitamins and hormonal imbalances. Protein should remain between 1.6-1.8 g/kg.

Useful supplements include creatine monohydrate (to increase muscle creatine phosphate stores), beta-alanine (to increase muscle carnosine), protein powder (if dietary protein is insufficient, especially in vegan diets), and dextrose (to promote insulin secretion, for example when taking creatine). The author notes that individual tolerance can vary significantly.
