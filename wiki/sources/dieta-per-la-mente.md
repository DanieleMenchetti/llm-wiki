# La dieta per la mente: quali cibi mangiare per mantenere il cervello in forma

**Source**: [raw/La dieta per la mente quali cibi mangiare per mantenere il cervello in forma.md](../raw/La%20dieta%20per%20la%20mente%20quali%20cibi%20mangiare%20per%20mantenere%20il%20cervello%20in%20forma.md)

## Key Takeaways
- Brain consumes 20% of total body energy
- Brain needs: omega-3, antioxidants, B vitamins, minerals, complex carbs
- Poor diet (saturated fats, refined sugars) causes inflammation and oxidative stress
- Brain foods: fatty fish (omega-3), nuts/seeds (vitamin E), colorful produce (flavonoids), whole grains/legumes, dark chocolate, EVO oil
- Fatty fish: eat at least twice weekly for memory support
- Nuts/seeds: protect nerve cells from aging, improve brain plasticity
- Colorful fruits/vegetables: flavonoids and carotenoids slow neuron wear
- Whole grains/legumes: complex carbs provide steady energy, prevent concentration drops
- Limit salt: excess can trigger brain inflammation and raise blood pressure
- Dark chocolate: stimulates endorphins, improves concentration and mood
- EVO oil: protects blood vessels, improves cerebral circulation
- Mediterranean diet is the ideal model for brain health
- Lifestyle: hydration, regular sleep, exercise (daily walk) essential

## Summary

This article emphasizes that what we eat affects not only our body but also mental clarity and memory. The brain consumes about 20% of total body energy and requires specific nutrients: good fatty acids, antioxidants, B vitamins, minerals, and complex sugars. A diet rich in saturated fats and refined sugars can trigger inflammation and oxidative stress, damaging nerve cells.

The article identifies key "brain foods":

- **Fatty fish** (blue fish and salmon): Rich in omega-3 fatty acids, fundamental for building brain cell membranes and nerve signal transmission. Omega-3s are "food for memory," helping brain cells communicate better and supporting memory function. Should be included at least twice weekly.

- **Nuts and oily seeds** (walnuts, almonds, flax seeds, chia seeds): Provide good fats, vitamin E, and magnesium. A handful protects nerve cells from aging and improves brain plasticity — "forever young."

- **Colorful fruits and vegetables** (blueberries, strawberries, spinach, cabbage, carrots): Contain flavonoids and carotenoids, powerful antioxidants that fight free radicals and slow neuron wear.

- **Whole grains and legumes** (whole bread/pasta, rice, oats, quinoa, buckwheat, legumes): Provide complex carbs that release energy slowly and gradually, maintaining stable blood sugar levels and ensuring constant fuel for the brain, avoiding concentration drops typical of rapid sugars.

- **Dark chocolate**: Contains flavonoids and stimulates endorphin production, favoring concentration and good mood. Rule: "little, but good."

- **EVO oil**: Pillar of Mediterranean diet, protects blood vessels and improves cerebral circulation, facilitating oxygen and nutrient delivery to nerve cells.

The article warns against excess salt, which can trigger inflammatory brain processes and directly raise blood pressure. It recommends using little salt since food already contains necessary sapidity.

The Mediterranean diet is confirmed as the winning model for brain health: varied, balanced, rich in vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish, and olive oil. However, diet alone is not enough — hydration and lifestyle are also fundamental. Drinking abundant water maintains efficient nerve impulse transmission, while regular sleep and physical exercise (like a daily walk) stimulate production of new brain connections.
