# I 10 migliori alimenti per la massa muscolare

**Source**: [raw/I 10 migliori alimenti per la massa muscolare.md](../raw/I%2010%20migliori%20alimenti%20per%20la%20massa%20muscolare.md)
**Date**: 2025-07-18
**Type**: Article
**Author**: Valentina Idà

## Key Takeaways
- Foods alone don't build muscle — need hypercaloric diet + serious training
- Top 10 foods: white meat, fatty fish, nuts, Greek yogurt, legumes, eggs, brown rice, dark chocolate, potatoes, EVO oil
- White meat (chicken): 27g protein per 100g, low fat
- Fatty fish (salmon, tuna): 19-22g protein per 100g, rich in omega-3
- Nuts: balance of carbs, proteins, healthy fats
- Legumes: soy (37g), lentils (23g), chickpeas (21g) protein per 100g dry
- Eggs: limit to 3-5 per week per SINU guidelines
- Brown rice: complex carbs, B vitamins, vitamin E
- Dark chocolate (70%+): 20-30g post-workout for energy
- Potatoes: complex carbs, potassium, easily digestible
- EVO oil: healthy monounsaturated fats
- Supplements (if needed): protein powder, prioritize food first

## Summary

This article outlines the 10 best foods for supporting muscle growth, emphasizing that foods alone cannot build muscle mass — a controlled caloric surplus and serious training are essential. The foundation is reaching a hypercaloric diet that allows gradual weight increase over weeks.

The 10 recommended foods are:

1. **White meat** (chicken, turkey, rabbit): Excellent protein source with low fat content. 100g chicken provides 27g protein. White meat can be consumed 2-3 times per week.

2. **Fatty fish** (salmon, tuna, mackerel, herring, sardines): Rich in omega-3. 100g tuna contains ~22g protein, salmon ~19g protein. Should be included regularly.

3. **Nuts** (walnuts, almonds, pistachios, cashews, pecans): Balance of carbs, proteins, and healthy fats. Rich in vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants. Support muscle construction and recovery.

4. **Greek yogurt**: High protein content, ideal for breakfast or snacks. Best paired with fresh fruit and seeds.

5. **Legumes** (soy, lentils, chickpeas): Valuable source of fiber, vitamins, minerals, carbs, and vegetable proteins. Soy (37g), lentils (23g), chickpeas (21g) protein per 100g dry. Note: vegetable proteins have lower biological value than animal proteins due to missing some essential amino acids.

6. **Eggs**: Especially egg whites for high-protein pancakes. SINU guidelines recommend maximum 3-5 eggs per week for balanced nutrition.

7. **Brown rice**: Complex carbs of high quality, rich in B vitamins (thiamine, niacin, pyridoxine, folate) for energy metabolism and nervous system health. Also contains vitamin E (antioxidant).

8. **Dark chocolate** (70%+ cocoa): 20-30g portion post-workout provides immediate energy from carbs and fats, aiding recovery. Rich in fiber, fats, and proteins.

9. **Potatoes** (classic and sweet): Rich in starch (complex carbs), fiber, vitamins C and B6, minerals. Potassium content helps maintain fluid balance and muscle recovery. Sweet potatoes offer more fiber, vitamin A, and antioxidants.

10. **EVO oil**: Ideal source of healthy monounsaturated fats. Best used raw or for light sautéing.

The article emphasizes that increasing muscle mass requires training (weight lifting with varied exercises and intensity) plus adequate rest (recovery is when protein synthesis and growth occur). Nutritionally, adequate protein intake and controlled caloric surplus are essential. Supplements like protein powder can help when diet doesn't provide adequate nutrients, but food should always be prioritized. Consistency and discipline are essential — results take time.
